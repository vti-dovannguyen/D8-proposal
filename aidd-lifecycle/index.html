Throughout
Customer Q&A and feedback — open at any point in Stages 1–5, never queued behind a gate.  |  Assumptions and limitations disclosed early — open questions, provisional decisions and known constraints are put in writing as soon as they appear.
When a gate fails: AI generates the fix tasks and the work re-enters that stage. Up to 2 automatic cycles per gate; beyond that a human decides the direction — send requirements back, adjust scope, or revisit the design.

Process shape

Stages 6   Key activities 22   Human decisions 10
Approval gates 3 (G1, G4, G5) · Automated gate 1 (G3) · Checkpoints 2 (G2, G6)

Human judgement is reserved for decisions that carry accountability. Anything a machine can verify sits behind an automated gate, so no one waits for a review a test could have answered. Automation reports facts; the QA report accepts risk — that part does not automate.

Tailoring

Gates apply per increment — a feature or a sprint — not once per project.

Small change or defect fix  G1 and G2 collapse into a single review; G3 and G4 always apply.
New feature  full path.

Anything not applicable is recorded as such, not silently skipped.

On automation scope: automated regression covers paths that have been specified and confirmed once; the first pass on any new feature is human. Coverage compounds sprint over sprint through the promotion step, so manual effort per feature falls — it does not reach zero.  |  Stage-specific tooling, environments and security terms are defined per engagement in the accompanying Process Definition Document.
